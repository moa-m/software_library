After the Rain

    The station clock was striking nine when the rain stopped. Beneath the awning of a small bookshop, drops from the tips of umbrellas left round marks on the paving stones. I folded my map, put it in my pocket, and set off along the river.

    My only plan was to write a letter at the cafe across the bridge. There was no need to hurry, and no reason to take the shortest route. Watching the sky brighten in the puddles, I turned down a street I usually walked past.

    There was a bench beside the river. Raindrops still clung to its back, but one end was dry. I sat down and opened my bag. A thin slip of paper fell from the book I had been reading. It was last week's shopping list.

    Milk, envelopes, blue thread. Those three brief notes brought back the kitchen that day, and even the light in the shop where I had stopped. A moment I had never meant to remember was still there on a small piece of paper.

    I had planned to write about something special. But with the blank page in front of me, it seemed all right to begin with this morning's rain. The colour of the water under the bridge. The windows of shops that had not opened yet. Perhaps there was no need to go far to find something worth sharing.

    Halfway across the bridge, someone approached from the other side, pushing a bicycle. I stepped aside at the narrowest point. They nodded and said, "It looks as though the sun is coming out." I nodded too, and looked up at a break in the clouds. A few words had changed the shape of the morning.

    Two white cups stood by the cafe window. The owner pulled out a chair and brought a basket for my damp bag. I ordered a pot of hot tea and spread the first sheet of writing paper on the table.

    I wrote, "How are you?" Then I put down my pen for a while. I had thought there was so much to say, yet the first line was always short. Outside the window, people were already carrying their umbrellas over their arms.

    "This morning I walked beside the river." Once I had written that, the next words came slowly. The drops outside the bookshop. The shopping list on the bench. The person I had passed on the bridge. As I wrote, each ordinary scene became something I could hold on to.

    "There is no hurry to reply," I added at the end. I would be glad if the letter found a quiet moment in a busy day. Perhaps while you were sitting by a window, or waiting for the kettle to boil.

    Before sealing the envelope, I drew a little bridge on the back of the shopping list. It was not a very good drawing. Still, it made the right bookmark for today. I tucked it between the pages of the letter and finished my tea.

    When I left the cafe, the paving stones had begun to dry. I decided to take a different way home. My bag felt a little lighter as I walked towards the postbox on the corner.
